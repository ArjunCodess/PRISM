# PRISM v2 — implementation plan

Branch: `v2`  
PR title: `v2: measure conjunction information decay (horizon, floor, official test)`

**Goal.** Keep PRISM as a usable CDM forecasting model on the project surface, and as a citable measurement on the research surface: how much extra information exists in CDM history beyond persistence, how fast that information dies as TCA approaches, and why mean error is a floor-jump statistic rather than “typical events got more accurate.”

**Not a goal.** A nicer dashboard, a transformer flagship, beating the 2019 Kelvins ranking by peeking, claiming operational ISRO/NASA use, or turning the website into a paper.

**Architecture.** There is one selected policy, and two surfaces.

- **Project surface (frontend, API, exhibit).** This is the public product: a T−48 CDM model. A visitor uses it. They see a forecast of later reported `log10(Pc)`, a band or REVIEW REQUIRED, and a SHAP explanation in chart and plain language. The site is a conjunction-risk model, not a methods appendix.
- **Research surface (backend, experiments, `metrics.json`, `paper/`, README science).** This is the expanded project: leakage-safe splits, floor anatomy, residual targets, conformal coverage, official-test labels, hypotheses H1–H4. Judges who open the paper or the metrics bundle should see a study, not a copilot pitch.

The model selected on validation is the only inference artifact. Persistence, median, Ridge, and the 18 August ensemble remain **baselines in the metrics tables**, not a second live model and not a second website mode. New work (residual target, floor model, conformal intervals, official-test labels) is how we *choose* that single policy. It does not spawn a parallel “research-only” API.

**What goes where.** Science lands first in `ml/`, `metrics.json`, and `paper/`. The frontend gains a new number or widget only when that number is frozen **and** a visitor needs it to use the model (forecast, interval, explanation, one honest limitation). Deep tables, Wilcoxon tests, dilution probes, and official-test scoreboards stay on the research surface (paper, README results, laboratory appendix). Do not dump the paper onto the homepage. Do not hide the research behind a product slogan.

| Surface | What it is | What a person sees |
|---|---|---|
| Project | CDM forecasting model | API + website: later `log10(Pc)` forecast, band or REVIEW REQUIRED, SHAP |
| Research | Expanded study | `paper/`, `metrics.json`, README results, lab appendix: H1–H4, CIs, official test |

**Living paper.** `paper/main.tex`, `paper/references.bib`, and `paper/main.pdf` are first-class research files. After **every** phase: update the tex (and bib if a new citation landed), update `README.md` **results** so they match the tex (keep the README lead as the CDM model), then rebuild `paper/main.pdf`. A phase is not done if the PDF is stale.

**Tech stack.** Existing: Python 3.11, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, pytest, FastAPI, Next.js. Add: split-conformal (mapie or a 20-line implementation), bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon tests. Do not add a new frontend framework.

## What “best version” means

On the **project surface**, a working CDM model: cutoff-safe T−48 forecast, explanation, uncertainty that is labeled honestly.

On the **research surface**, a rerunnable experiment that can say, with intervals:

1. Extra information beyond copying the latest report is largest at T−72 and nearly gone at T−12.
2. Persistence median AE at T−48 is 0. Typical events often do not move. Mean error is dominated by rare jumps, especially collapses to the dataset floor `−30`.
3. That extra information does not automatically improve high-risk decisions. On the local split there are 9 positives. On ESA’s official test there are ~150 high-risk events — that is the split where ESA-style loss is estimable.
4. Bootstrap 90% bands cover ~48% of outcomes. Conformal intervals are the uncertainty claim.

Related work we must cite and distinguish:

- Uriot et al. 2021 — challenge design, LRP baseline, 12/96 teams beat it. They did not publish a multi-horizon information-decay table on a naturalistic split.
- Duncan, Wysack, Frisbee 2014 (AMOS) — physics Pc forecasting via expected covariance reduction. We are the public-data statistical analogue, not a Monte Carlo covariance propagator.
- Alfano — probability dilution: large covariance can make Pc look safely small. ESA already ships `max_risk_estimate`. Use the gap `max_risk − risk` as a dilution probe.
- Stauch / Olson et al. 2024–2026 (AMOS, *Journal of the Astronautical Sciences*) — predict whether covariance will stay large, for sensor tasking. Different target. Cite it; do not compete with it.
- NASA CARA notes — covariance contracts toward TCA; Pc often drops. That is the physical story behind horizon decay.

ESA released the official test **labels** on Zenodo (record 4463683, January 2021). v1 treats `test_data.csv` as unlabeled and only checks schema. v2 scores that external set **once**, after every modeling choice is frozen. Never train on it. Never tune on it.
